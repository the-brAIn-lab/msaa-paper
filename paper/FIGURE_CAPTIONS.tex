% Uncertainty definitions




\paragraph{Uncertainty definitions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SEM over decoder repetitions.} Used in Fig.~2 and S11. The timepoint-identification decoder is run 40 times with different random fold assignments, and the band is the standard error of the mean over those 40 repetitions. It reflects decoder fold variability on a fixed fit, not variability across participants or fits.
  \item \textbf{SD across fit seeds.} Used in Fig.~4A, 4B, 4D and S9 (and summarised as counts in Fig.~4C, S1, S3, S4--S6). Every MS-AA fit was repeated from 10 FurthestSum initializations (fit seeds 0--9) on the same data. The SD across seeds measures how sensitive a result is to initialization. \textbf{Fit seeds are not independent statistical samples}: they share the data, the participants and the held-out splits. We therefore report seed spread and seed
    consistency (for example ``10/10 seeds'') and do not run significance tests over seeds.
  \item \textbf{Participant-split variability.} Used in S2 and S10 (SEM over the 20 outer participant splits) and in the cross-fitted selection frequencies (Fig.~5, S5, S6). Each outer split divides the 36 participants into two halves of 18. Archetypes are ranked on one half and scored on the other, in both directions, giving 40 rankings per $K \times$ condition $\times$ seed. This variability reflects which participants are in the ranking and scoring halves. The 20 splits overlap, so their SEM is descriptive, not a formal standard error over independent samples.
\end{enumerate}
Variability over observations (Fig.~5 temporal profiles, Fig.~6B--C, S8A) is over \textbf{participant-condition observations}: one per participant per condition (108 scans, 36 per condition, from 87 unique participants; 21 participants contributed both an intact and a word-scrambled scan, and every other participant contributed one scan).

\paragraph{Conventions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Chance} for timepoint identification is 1/300 (300 timepoints per condition), drawn as a grey dashed line.
  \item \textbf{Tie rule.} When the decoder scores correlations, values within $10^{-8}$ of each other count as tied. The decoded timepoint is the first within $10^{-8}$ of the row maximum, and the rank counts entries $\leq$ diagonal $+\,10^{-8}$. This makes the exact ties that are common in temporal fits break the same way on every machine. All held-out numbers use this rule.
  \item \textbf{Held-out averaging.} For a given fit seed, the two directions (A$\to$B, B$\to$A) are averaged within each outer split, then the 20 splits are averaged. Multi-seed figures then average the 10 seeds.
  \item \textbf{Yeo-7} composition is \textbf{descriptive}: it reports the share of a motif's absolute spatial weight that falls in each Yeo-7 network. It is not an enrichment test, and no null model is implied.
\end{itemize}
